# North Star Metric

## Metric

**Weekly Active Users (WAUs) Asking Questions**

## Why This Metric?

This metric captures the core value loop of Prism:

1. User has a question
2. User asks Prism (instead of searching manually or asking a human)
3. Prism delivers a cited, confident answer
4. User saves time, feels empowered, returns

If users are asking questions every week, they're experiencing the product's core value. This metric is a leading indicator for:

- Feature stickiness (they keep coming back)
- Behavior change (Prism becomes their default for knowledge queries)
- Team collaboration (multiple users querying the same knowledge base)

## Target

**Primary:** Achieve **1,000 WAUs** asking ≥1 question/week by end of Q2 2026

**Growth milestones:**
- 100 WAUs by end of onboarding launch (Week 1)
- 500 WAUs by Week 6
- 1,000 WAUs by Week 12

## Supporting Metrics

| Metric | Target | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Time to First Answer | < 2 min | Proves onboarding delivers value fast |
| Onboarding Completion | > 60% | Funnel health — how many stick the landing |
| Day-7 Retention | > 40% | Do they come back after the aha moment? |
| Questions per WAU | > 3/week | Are they relying on Prism or just trying it? |
| Template Adoption | > 30% | Leading indicator for non-GitHub users |

## What Changes Our Mind

If we see high signup → low questioning, it means:
- The aha moment isn't strong enough
- The knowledge base isn't valuable enough
- The UX has too much friction

At that point, we'd revisit whether "questions asked" is the right North Star, or if we need to focus on "documents created" (authoring-first) or "team invite rate" (collaboration-first).
